About This Guide

This guide was designed specifically for family caregivers, professional care teams, and loved ones of persons living with dementia (PLwD) in Riverside and San Bernardino counties. It is not a general emergency guide — it addresses the unique challenges that cognitive impairment creates during environmental emergencies.

People with dementia may not recognize danger, may resist instructions, may wander, or may be unable to communicate symptoms like overheating or difficulty breathing. This guide gives caregivers practical, dementia-informed steps before, during, and after each type of emergency.

The Inland Empire spans urban valleys, deserts, foothills, and mountain communities. Extreme heat, wildfire, smoke, flash flooding, winter weather, and power shutoffs vary sharply by location, so caregivers should register for the alert system serving each county and city where care occurs.

📌 How to Use This Guide Use the tabs above to navigate by hazard type. Each section includes: (1) Why this hazard is especially dangerous for PLwD, (2) dementia-specific tips BEFORE, DURING, and AFTER the event, and (3) links to Inland Empire agencies. The Inland Empire Resources tab lists all local agencies with contact information.
🌐 Language Access: Many Inland Empire agencies offer services in Spanish, Vietnamese, Korean, Chinese, and other languages. When calling, ask: "Do you have services in [language]?" / "¿Tienen servicios en español?"

Why Dementia Changes Everything in an Emergency

Understanding unique vulnerabilities

🌡️

Cannot Self-Regulate Temperature

PLwD often cannot sense or communicate that they are overheating or dangerously cold. They may refuse to drink water or remove heavy clothing during heat emergencies.

🚨

Cannot Follow Evacuation Instructions

Complex multi-step evacuation instructions are extremely difficult for PLwD to follow. Sirens, crowds, and displacement cause intense confusion, fear, and agitation.

💊

Medication Management Risk

Many dementia medications require refrigeration or consistent schedules. Power outages and displacement put medication continuity at serious risk.

🚶

Wandering & Elopement Risk

Environmental disruptions dramatically increase wandering. Smoke, loud noises, and unfamiliar surroundings can trigger a PLwD to leave a safe space.

😟

Behavioral Symptoms Worsen

Anxiety, agitation, depression, and aggression all intensify during emergencies and displacement — even days or weeks after the event ends.

🫁

Heightened Respiratory & Cardiac Risk

Dementia-affected older adults commonly have cardiovascular and respiratory conditions that are severely worsened by wildfire smoke and extreme heat.

Year-Round Preparedness for Dementia Households

Steps to take NOW — before any emergency occurs

💡 Dementia Caregiver Tip Involve the person with dementia in preparedness activities when possible and when they are calm. Familiar routines and familiar faces are the most powerful tools during any emergency. Practice and repetition now make emergencies less frightening later.

✅ Essential Emergency Preparedness Checklist for PLwD Households

✔IDCreate a laminated ID card for the PLwD with name, address, emergency contacts, diagnoses, and medications — keep one on their person at all times.
✔MedicalCompile a "Go Bag" medical folder: copies of all prescriptions, medication list with dosages, physician contacts, insurance cards, and advance directives.
✔MedicalMaintain a 7–14 day supply of all medications; ask your pharmacist and health plan about emergency refill options.
✔SafetyAsk local law enforcement about wandering-response programs and consider MedicAlert identification and 24/7 support.
✔SafetyPlace door alarms and window sensors to prevent elopement; consider a GPS tracking device worn by the PLwD.
✔PlanCreate a written Personal Emergency Plan with at least 2 backup contacts who know the PLwD's needs, triggers, and calming strategies.
✔PlanIdentify two evacuation destinations: one local (friend/family) and one further away (motel, shelter) that is dementia-aware.
✔AlertsRiverside County residents: register for Alert RivCo. San Bernardino County residents: register for TENS/Ready SB. Also check for a city-specific alert system.
✔UtilitiesContact SoCalGas and Southern California Edison to enroll in the Medical Baseline Program for reduced rates and priority notification during outages.
✔ComfortPack a "comfort bag" with familiar objects: a favorite photo album, a soft blanket, a cherished item, and a playlist of favorite music to reduce agitation during displacement.
✔HomeInstall a backup battery for medical devices (CPAP, nebulizer, etc.) and keep a manual can opener, flashlights, and battery-powered fan/radio.
✔NeighborsTell trusted neighbors about the PLwD so they can check in during emergencies. Post a "PLwD lives here" notice inside a door for first responders if comfortable.
✔PetsIdentify pet-friendly shelters and boarding; a familiar pet is a powerful calming resource for PLwD — do not leave pets behind.

🎒 Go-Bag Essentials for Dementia Households

💊14-day medication supply + list
🪪ID card + insurance cards
📄Medical records & advance directives
💧Water (1 gallon/person/day × 3 days)
🍱Non-perishable easy-to-eat foods
🩺Blood pressure cuff & thermometer
😷N95 masks (wildfire/air quality)
🧴Incontinence supplies
🔦Flashlight + batteries + radio
🖼️Comfort items: photos, music, blanket
🔋Phone charger + backup battery
💰Cash in small bills

Extreme Heat

Dementia-specific guidance for heat emergencies in Inland Empire

🚨 Critical Risk for PLwD Persons with dementia often cannot feel or communicate heat stress. Many dementia medications (antipsychotics, diuretics, anticholinergics) impair the body's ability to regulate temperature and increase heat stroke risk dramatically. Do NOT wait for the PLwD to ask for help — monitor continuously.
💡 Dementia Caregiver Tip If the PLwD resists going to a cooling center, reframe it: "Let's go get a cool drink" or "We're going to visit somewhere with a nice breeze." Use simple, positive, one-step instructions. Arguing or explaining the heat danger often increases resistance.

✅ BEFORE a Heat Event Prepare

  • Know your PLwD's heat-related medications — ask their doctor about heat risks
  • Identify the nearest Inland Empire cooling center (see Inland Empire Resources tab)
  • Install window AC or fans; check functionality before summer
  • Keep curtains/blinds closed on south/west-facing windows during daytime
  • Register for Alert RivCo or San Bernardino County TENS/Ready SB, depending on the address, plus any city-specific alert system
  • Ensure caregiver respite backup for prolonged heat events
  • Enroll PLwD in SoCal Edison Medical Baseline for energy cost help
  • Have electrolyte drinks and water stored and accessible

⚠️ DURING a Heat Event Act

  • Check temperature and signs of heat illness every 30–60 minutes: flushed skin, confusion, dry mouth, fast heartbeat
  • Offer water and cool drinks every 15–20 minutes — do not wait for them to ask
  • Use cool, damp cloths on neck, wrists, and forehead; avoid sudden cold shocks
  • Keep indoors in AC; if no AC — go to a cooling center or mall
  • Avoid strenuous activity between 10 AM – 4 PM
  • Watch for heat stroke: hot/dry skin, confusion worsening, loss of consciousness → Call 911
  • Keep blinds closed; use fans + bowls of ice for low-tech cooling
  • Dress in loose, light-colored clothing — assist PLwD in changing if needed

🔄 AFTER a Heat Event Recover

  • Monitor for delayed dehydration symptoms for 24–48 hours after heat breaks
  • Check medications: were any doses missed or skipped? Contact provider to adjust
  • Watch for increased confusion or behavioral changes — these can persist for days
  • Report any heat illness symptoms to their physician, even if mild
  • Re-establish normal routines as quickly as possible to reduce ongoing agitation
  • Debrief: what worked? Update your emergency plan

Air Pollution & Poor Air Quality

Protecting PLwD from smoke, smog, and hazardous air days

🚨 Critical Risk for PLwD Older adults with dementia often have underlying COPD, asthma, or heart disease. They cannot reliably report breathing difficulty or chest pain. Wildfire smoke, ozone, and particulate matter can trigger rapid health decline. Never assume a PLwD is "fine" because they haven't complained.
💡 Dementia Caregiver Tip PLwD may try to go outside despite poor air quality. Redirect with indoor activities: folding laundry, sorting objects, looking at photo albums, or watching familiar TV shows. Gently close doors and explain in simple terms: "The air outside is smoky today — let's stay cozy inside."

✅ BEFORE Prepare

  • Purchase N95 or KN95 masks in the PLwD's correct size — practice wearing them when calm
  • Get a HEPA air purifier for the bedroom and main living area
  • Seal gaps around doors/windows with towels or weather stripping during smoke events
  • Know the PLwD's respiratory medications and keep inhalers stocked
  • Bookmark AirNow.gov and SCAQMD's AQI alerts; subscribe to SCAQMD email alerts
  • Ask their doctor: "At what AQI level should we keep them strictly indoors?"

⚠️ DURING Poor Air Quality Act

  • Keep all windows and doors tightly closed
  • Run the HEPA air purifier on high in rooms where PLwD spends time
  • Set HVAC to recirculate (not fresh air intake)
  • If PLwD must go outside briefly, assist them in wearing an N95 mask
  • Watch for: increased coughing, wheezing, shortness of breath, chest tightness, new or worsening confusion — these are signs of smoke exposure
  • Do NOT use candles, fireplaces, or fry foods — these worsen indoor air
  • Keep PLwD hydrated — dry air and smoke increase dehydration risk
  • AQI 150+ (Unhealthy): strict indoor stay for all PLwD
    AQI 200+ (Very Unhealthy): consider evacuation to cleaner air location

🔄 AFTER Recover

  • Ventilate home only after AQI returns below 100
  • Wipe down surfaces with damp cloth — smoke particles settle on furniture
  • Replace HEPA air purifier filters as directed
  • Report any new respiratory symptoms to the physician — even mild ones
  • Monitor for cognitive changes or behavioral decline for several days
  • Wash all bedding and clothing exposed to smoke

Wildfire & Evacuation

Dementia-specific wildfire preparedness and evacuation guidance for Inland Empire

🚨 Critical Risk for PLwD PLwD cannot reliably follow multi-step evacuation orders. Sirens, smoke, and emergency personnel in uniforms or respirators are terrifying and disorienting. Behavioral symptoms — including combativeness and refusal to leave — are common. Caregivers must have a practiced, simple plan. Never wait until a mandatory evacuation is issued — evacuate at the WARNING stage.
💡 Dementia Caregiver Tip Practice a simplified "evacuation routine" during calm times so it feels familiar. Use one calm, reassuring phrase such as: "It's time for our trip. Let's get in the car." Avoid words like "fire," "danger," or "evacuate" — these trigger panic. Bring the comfort bag, a familiar blanket, and any beloved pet.

✅ BEFORE Wildfire Season Prepare

  • Sign up for Alert RivCo or San Bernardino County TENS/Ready SB
  • Know two evacuation routes and follow official county and city alerts for current evacuation maps and orders
  • Pre-identify a dementia-aware evacuation destination — inform them in advance
  • Practice a "no-discussion" departure routine — pre-pack the car with the Go-Bag
  • Call 211 for current shelter information and explain all dementia, mobility, communication, medical-equipment, and caregiver needs
  • Keep the car's gas tank at half full or more during fire season
  • Clear dry brush/vegetation within 30 feet of your home
  • Alert neighbors about the PLwD — ask them to check in if they see smoke
  • Have a secondary caregiver designated who knows the plan

⚠️ DURING a Wildfire Threat Act

  • Evacuate EARLY — do not wait for mandatory order; PLwD evacuation takes more time
  • Use your rehearsed calm phrase: "It's time for our trip." Do not explain the fire
  • Dress PLwD in long sleeves and closed-toe shoes — pack their comfort bag
  • Take all medications, medical equipment, and the Go-Bag folder
  • Alert 911 if the PLwD refuses to leave — mention cognitive impairment for AFN assistance
  • Follow designated evacuation routes only — do not improvise routes
  • Once evacuated, maintain PLwD's routine as closely as possible: same meal times, same sleep schedule, familiar music
  • If sheltering, ask for a quiet area away from crowds and alarms

🔄 AFTER Returning Home Recover

  • Do NOT return until officially cleared — ash contains toxic heavy metals and carcinogens
  • Wear N95 masks during cleanup; keep PLwD away from cleanup activities
  • Expect significant behavioral changes for weeks after displacement — increase patience, structure, and reassurance
  • Re-establish home routines immediately; familiar surroundings are deeply stabilizing
  • Contact the Alzheimer's Association California Southland Chapter helpline for post-disaster caregiver support
  • Check with physician: were medications maintained? Any new health concerns?
  • Document losses for FEMA/insurance. The Alzheimer's Association California Southland Chapter can help navigate claims.

Flooding & Flash Floods

Flood preparedness for dementia-affected households in Inland Empire

🚨 Critical Risk for PLwD PLwD may not understand the danger of floodwater (6 inches of moving water can knock down an adult). They may wander into flooded areas, resist evacuation, or become extremely agitated by loud rain and unfamiliar sounds. Begin evacuation preparations well before conditions become dangerous.
💡 Dementia Caregiver Tip Rain sounds, thunder, and water intrusion into the home are highly disorienting for PLwD. Use a calm voice, soothing music, and a "story" framing: "We're going on a little adventure to stay at [name's] house tonight." Avoid showing them floodwater if possible — visual exposure to flood conditions worsens panic.

✅ BEFORE Flood Season Prepare

  • Know your flood zone — check FEMA Flood Map: msc.fema.gov
  • Move medications, medical records, and important documents to upper floors or a waterproof bag
  • Purchase flood insurance if in a flood zone — standard homeowners insurance does NOT cover flooding
  • Identify ground-floor hazards — PLwD may try to walk in water or touch electrical outlets near floodwater
  • Know two evacuation routes from your home/neighborhood
  • Elevate CPAP machines, nebulizers, and other electrical medical equipment
  • Keep non-slip mats and clear pathways in case of partial flooding

⚠️ DURING a Flood Event Act

  • Monitor RivCo Ready or Ready SB County along with National Weather Service flash-flood warnings
  • Never allow PLwD near floodwater — all floodwater is contaminated
  • Turn off electricity at the breaker if water is entering the home
  • Move to the highest floor if unable to evacuate; call 911
  • When evacuating: use your calm reassurance phrase; carry them or use a wheelchair on elevated ground
  • Do not use elevators during active flooding
  • Pack Go-Bag, medications, and comfort items before water rises
  • Contact 211 for nearest shelter accepting AFN individuals

🔄 AFTER Flooding Recover

  • Do NOT return until health authorities declare the home safe — mold grows within 24–48 hours
  • Keep PLwD away from mold-damaged areas — mold spores cause respiratory harm
  • Check all medications — discard any that were exposed to floodwater
  • Watch for increased agitation, confusion, or behavioral symptoms — displacement stress is prolonged
  • Apply for FEMA Disaster Assistance: disasterassistance.gov or call 1-800-621-3362
  • Contact the resident's county aging or social-services department for emergency housing, food, IHSS, and financial-assistance referrals
  • Re-establish familiar routines as soon as safely possible

Extended Power Outages

Protecting PLwD during prolonged electrical failures

🚨 Critical Risk for PLwD Power outages disrupt medication refrigeration, medical devices (CPAP, oxygen, nebulizers), lighting, and temperature control simultaneously. Darkness and unfamiliar sounds cause extreme disorientation for PLwD. Even a short outage can trigger prolonged behavioral distress.
💡 Dementia Caregiver Tip Keep battery-powered nightlights throughout the home year-round — familiar light sources reduce nighttime confusion during outages. A battery-powered music player with familiar, calming music is one of the most powerful tools to reduce agitation when all other systems fail.

✅ BEFORE an Outage Prepare

  • Enroll in SoCal Edison Medical Baseline Program for reduced rates and priority restoration
  • Register with SCE's Medical Needs program if PLwD uses life-sustaining equipment
  • Have a 7-day refrigerated medication plan — ask pharmacist about storage temperatures and options during outages
  • Purchase a backup battery or generator for medical devices (CPAP, oxygen concentrator)
  • Keep battery-powered nightlights, flashlights, and lanterns charged and accessible
  • Have a manual medication list and dosage schedule printed (not just on phone)
  • Stock non-perishable, easy-prep foods that require no cooking

⚠️ DURING an Outage Act

  • Immediately activate battery-powered lighting — darkness is severely disorienting for PLwD
  • Check medication refrigeration — most insulin requires refrigeration; call pharmacist if unsure
  • Monitor temperature: if home exceeds 90°F → go to a cooling center or friend's home
  • Keep routines as normal as possible — same meal times, same activities
  • Use battery-powered music player with familiar music to reduce anxiety
  • Do NOT use generators, grills, or camp stoves indoors — carbon monoxide risk
  • Report medical equipment needs to SCE: (800) 655-4555
  • Keep mobile phone charged via car charger or battery bank

🔄 AFTER Power Restores Recover

  • Check all refrigerated medications — discard if temperature was >46°F for >2 hours
  • Replace any food that may have spoiled — do not feed PLwD questionable food items
  • Restart all medical devices and confirm proper functioning
  • Watch for behavioral symptoms that may linger for days after the disruption
  • Contact physician if any medications were missed or refrigeration was compromised
  • Replenish emergency supplies and recharge all backup batteries
