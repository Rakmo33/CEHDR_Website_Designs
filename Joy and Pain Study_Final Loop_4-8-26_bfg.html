Introduction & Purpose

Why This Study?

  • Black Americans have the highest risk of Alzheimer's disease and dementia in the U.S.
  • Genetics and family history do not explain this disparity
  • Black people also have the highest prevalence of chronic stress and untreated pain
  • Both stress and pain cause inflammation in the body
  • Inflammation increases your risk for poor brain health

Knowledge Gap

Poor representation of Black Americans in Alzheimer's research has led to gaps in understanding the biological and social-ecological factors driving their increased dementia risk.


Chronic stress impacts the HPA axis, leading to persistent inflammation and increased dementia risk. A meta-analysis of 300+ studies confirmed chronic stress increases proinflammatory cytokines.

Study Aims

Aim 1: Characterize the association between stress and cognitive status in Black American adults.


Aim 2: Characterize the direct and interactive effects of stress and pain on cognitive impairment.

The Research Roadmap
Starting from chronic stress, we travel through pain and healthcare experiences to arrive at brain health outcomes.

🟡 Question 1: Chronic Stress

How does chronic stress affect brain health?


Long-term stress keeps the body on high alert and can weaken memory, focus, and decision making.

🟡 Question 2: Stress + Pain

How are chronic stress and pain related?


Stress can increase pain, pain can increase stress, and together they place greater strain on the brain. Long-term pain can increase inflammation, which may interfere with memory, attention, and clear thinking.

🟡 Question 3: Healthcare

Can interactions with your doctor protect or harm your brain health?


Feeling heard and respected in healthcare can help manage stress and pain and support brain health.

🏁 End Goal: Brain Health

Does chronic stress + pain + healthcare experience = brain health?


Brain health includes memory, attention, and problem solving that support everyday functioning.

How We Conducted the Study
1

Used a survey design to collect data on stress, experience with pain, and brain health. Used the OraSure saliva sample to collect data to measure inflammation.

2

186 Black Americans were recruited and received $75 for participating. Eight data collection events: two in Riverside, two in Santa Ana, and four in Los Angeles.

3

103 women and 83 men in the sample. Average age is 65. Youngest participant is 45 years old; oldest is 92 years old.

4

Survey data downloaded and transferred to the software program R for analysis. Saliva samples were frozen and then analyzed in a lab at UC Irvine (UCI IISBR).

5

All data were de-identified: responses to survey questions and saliva samples are not connected to personal information.

Data collection locations: Second Baptist Church (Orange County) | Alpha House (Los Angeles County) | Victoria Manor (Riverside County)
What Do the Numbers Mean?

Brain Health (MMSE)

Clinical tool: Mini-Mental State Exam. Scores range 0-30. Higher scores = better brain health. Lower scores = mild or severe cognitive impairment.

Chronic Stress Scale

NIH-recommended tool with 51 items measuring financial issues, work, love and marriage, family, children, and social life. Higher scores = higher chronic stress. Score range: 0-150.

Pain Measures

Pain intensity: severity from no pain to worst imaginable. Pain interference: how much pain disrupts daily life. Also measured adequate treatment of pain by provider.

Inflammation from Saliva

Proinflammatory cytokines measured: CRP, IL-1β, IL-6, TNF-α. Green = Low | Orange = Moderate | Red = High inflammation. Analyzed at UCI IISBR by Lab Manager Michael Chang.

Doctor-Patient Relationship (IPC)

Interpersonal Processes of Care Index measures: communication, patient-centered decision making, and interpersonal style (compassion, respect, discrimination).

Stress by Sex

No significant difference in total chronic stress scores between women and men. Specific stressors (work, relationships, family, money, parenting) may differ by sex.
