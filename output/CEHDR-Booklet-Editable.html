PACK / PLAN / PREPARE
Using this booklet
Pack for the person living with dementia and
the caregiver. Check each box as you
prepare. Keep familiar items and the supplies
you normally use.
Some supplies stay at home; others go in a
bag you can carry. Keep important records
secure and accessible to a trusted caregiver.
The separate “This Is Me” insert shares the
person’s story, routines and support needs. It
does not replace the laminated ID card,
medicine list or care documents.
Review the kit every 6 months and after
changes in care. Ask your care team about
medicines, diet, swallowing and medical
equipment.
In immediate danger, call 911.

CEHDR | Emergency preparedness 4
PACK / PLAN / PREPARE
Medicines & medical
supplies
Aim for a 7- to 14-day supply of ALL
prescription medicines. Ask your
prescriber or pharmacist what is allowed
and how to build the supply. Rotate it to
keep it current.
Written medication list with drug names,
dosages, frequency, and prescribing
physician
Ask the pharmacist which nonprescription
medicines are safe: pain reliever, antacid,
antihistamine and anti-diarrheal. Pack only
medicines approved for the person.
Insect repellent and sunscreen
Thermometer (to monitor for heat illness
and fever)
Blood pressure cuff and pulse oximeter

CEHDR | Emergency preparedness 9
PACK / PLAN / PREPARE
Dementia support & comfort
Favorite photograph album or printed
family photos (reduces disorientation and
anxiety)
A beloved or familiar comfort object
(stuffed animal, keepsake, familiar toy)
Battery-powered music player pre-loaded
with the person with dementia's favorite
familiar music (one of the most powerful
calming tools during displacement and
power outages)
A simple, familiar activity: puzzle, soft
fidget toy, sorting objects, deck of cards
Small notebook with the person with
dementia's life story summary ("This Is
Me" card) to share with shelter staff or first
responders

PACK / PLAN / PREPARE
Dementia support & comfort
Continued
List of the person with dementia's known
triggers (sounds, phrases, situations that
cause agitation) and known calming
strategies - for use by anyone providing
care
List of the person with dementia's daily
routine (wake time, meal preferences,

Small whiteboard or notepad for simple

communication is impaired
If used, keep the person’s wearable GPS

and Safe and Found wandering support.

with that service.
CEHDR | Emergency preparedness 11
PACK / PLAN / PREPARE
Food & water
Water: minimum 1 gallon per person per
day for at least 3 days (include extra for
medication administration)
Electrolyte drinks or packets, such as
Pedialyte or Gatorade powder, if
appropriate for the person’s care plan.
People with dementia may not ask for
water. If fluids are restricted, ask the care
team for guidance.
Non-perishable, easy-to-eat foods that
require no cooking or preparation: nut
butter, crackers, applesauce pouches,
canned fruit, granola bars, instant oatmeal,
trail mix Choose versions that need no
cooking; follow preparation instructions
and swallowing guidance.

CEHDR | Emergency preparedness 13
PACK / PLAN / PREPARE
Air quality & wildfire smoke
N95 or KN95 masks - properly fitted for
both person with dementia and caregiver
(practice wearing ahead of time)
Portable HEPA air purifier
(compact/travel-sized) with extra filters
Sealed ziplock bags to store clean masks
and prevent smoke contamination
Laminated AirNow.gov quick-reference
card with the AQI color scale and activity
guidance. Use the official printable guides
at airnow.gov/aqi/aqi-basics and
airnow.gov/activity-guides. Ask the care
team which guidance fits the person.

CEHDR | Emergency preparedness 17
PACK / PLAN / PREPARE
Power outage supplies
Continued
Backup power for medical equipment,
such as CPAP or an oxygen concentrator:
plan with the equipment provider.
Fuel-burning generators must run
outdoors, at least 20 feet from doors,
windows and vents. Follow manufacturer
instructions for battery power stations.
Manual medication log (printed, not just on
phone) with full schedule and dosages
Cooler and ice packs for refrigerated
medications during outages

CEHDR | Emergency preparedness 22
PACK / PLAN / PREPARE
Key caregiver reminders
Review and update the kit every 6 months.
Rotate medications and food to keep
supplies current.
Register with AlertOC before an
emergency through
ocgov.com/about-county/emergency. Ask
OC Emergency Management about
current AFN services or registry
availability.
Enroll in SoCal Edison's Medical Baseline
Program if the person with dementia uses
powered medical equipment: (800)
655-4555.
Ask MedicAlert about medical ID and Safe
and Found wandering support:
800-432-5378 or medicalert.org. Confirm
enrollment, fees and service details. This
number is for member-service questions;
call 911 if a person is missing.
