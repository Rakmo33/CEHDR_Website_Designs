“Health equity is not achieved through research alone. It is achieved when research is informed by community voices, translated into meaningful action, and used to create opportunities for all people to live healthier lives. At CEHDR, we are committed to building that bridge between science and community.”

Honoring Community Voices, Advancing Health Equity

As we celebrate Juneteenth and reflect on the ongoing pursuit of equity, I am reminded that meaningful change begins by listening, learning, and working alongside communities. At CEHDR, that commitment continues to guide everything we do.

This issue highlights an exciting season of growth for our Center. You'll meet some of our newest team members, explore our newly launched website and expanded social media presence, and learn more about the community partnerships and research initiatives helping to advance environmental health equity across Southern California.

We are also proud to feature highlights from our Joy & Pain study, which examines how social determinants and environmental experiences shape health and well-being among Black Americans. Through our Citizen Panels, the CEHDR RISE Fellowship Program, and other community-engaged initiatives, we continue to create opportunities for community members, researchers, and students to learn from one another, co-create solutions to pressing health challenges, and strengthen the next generation of leaders committed to health equity and environmental justice.

None of this work would be possible without the support of our community partners, students, faculty affiliates, and supporters, including the Pacific Life Foundation. Together, we are building a future where research is more inclusive, more responsive, and more impactful.

Thank you for being part of the CEHDR community. I invite you to explore this issue, connect with us online, and join us as we continue advancing research, education, and action for healthier communities.

— Karen D. Lincoln, PhD, MSW, MA, FGSA, FAASWSW

Director, Center for Environmental Health Disparities Research
