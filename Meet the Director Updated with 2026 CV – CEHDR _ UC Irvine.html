Who She Is

Driven by Equity. Grounded in Community.

Professor & Director, CEHDR · Joe C. Wen School of Population & Public Health · UC Irvine

Dr. Karen D. Lincoln is a Professor in the Department of Environmental and Occupational Health and Director of the Center for Environmental Health Disparities Research (CEHDR) at the Joe C. Wen School of Population & Public Health, University of California, Irvine. She is deeply committed to eliminating environmental racism and advancing policies that promote health, equity, and justice for historically marginalized communities.

Dr. Lincoln holds a BA (Honors) in Sociology from the University of California, Berkeley; an MSW and MA from the University of Michigan; and a PhD in Social Work and Sociology, also from the University of Michigan. She is a Fellow of the Gerontological Society of America (FGSA) and a Fellow of the American Academy of Social Work and Social Welfare (FAASWSW, 2026).

Her Research

Where Science Meets Social Justice

Social & Environmental Determinants of Health · Aging · Health Disparities

With a distinguished career spanning sociology, social work, gerontology, and public health, Dr. Lincoln's research examines the social and environmental determinants of health — with a focus on the disproportionate burden of exposures on Black and other underserved communities. She champions community-driven solutions to ensure those most impacted have a voice in shaping policy.

Her recent and ongoing work extends into environmental health equity, examining how historical redlining, air pollution, and neighborhood-level exposures affect the mental and physical health of Black Americans. She leads studies on sleep health and brain aging, Alzheimer's disease disparities, racial discrimination and accelerated biological aging, and climate resilience for dementia-affected households. Dr. Lincoln is the Principal Investigator on an NIH/NINDS-funded project to develop a Black aging cohort in Southern California using social determinants of health data.

Her multidisciplinary approach has produced interventions addressing disparities in healthcare access, environmental exposures, and aging-related inequities — working with local organizations, policymakers, and public health officials to drive lasting systemic change across California and beyond.

CEHDR Leadership

Leading Multidisciplinary Research for Systemic Change

Director since 2023 · Joe C. Wen School of Population & Public Health

Dr. Lincoln leads a multidisciplinary team of researchers, community partners, and policymakers to develop sustainable interventions that improve health outcomes and drive systemic change across California and beyond. At CEHDR, Dr. Lincoln's work is rooted in a simple but powerful belief: that healthier, more just communities are possible when research, policy, and lived experience come together.

In addition to directing CEHDR, Dr. Lincoln serves as Co-Chair of the Environmental Justice Advisory Council (EJAC) at the California Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC), State of California (2024–2026), and as a Standing Member of the Healthcare and Health Disparities Study Section (HHD) at the National Institutes of Health (2024–2028).

Founder, AAAE

Advocates for African American Elders

Founded 2012 · Center for Environmental Health Disparities Research, UC Irvine

Dr. Lincoln founded Advocates for African American Elders (AAAE) — now housed at CEHDR, UC Irvine — a Los Angeles, Riverside, and Orange County organization dedicated to improving health, well-being, and quality of life for Black older adults through education, advocacy, and policy reform. AAAE engages community members directly in health education and research, ensuring that lived experience informs scientific and policy work.

Mentor & Educator

Cultivating the Next Generation

Teaching · Mentorship · Doctoral Training

A celebrated teacher and trainer, Dr. Lincoln has guided countless students and early-career researchers, cultivating a diverse, interdisciplinary workforce committed to advancing social and environmental justice. She currently teaches graduate courses on Environmental Health Disparities and Health Status and Care Disparities at UC Irvine, and mentors CEHDR Rise Fellows, doctoral students, and postdoctoral scholars.

She has served as a mentor through the NIH-funded Program to Increase Diversity in Behavior Medicine and Sleep Research (PRIDE) and has chaired and served on numerous dissertation committees at UCI, USC, and other institutions nationwide.
