 Who She Is

Driven by Equity. Grounded in Community.

Professor & Director, CEHDR · Joe C. Wen School of Population & Public Health · UC Irvine

Dr. Karen D. Lincoln is Professor of Environmental and Occupational Health and Director of the Center for Environmental Health Disparities Research at the Joe C. Wen School of Population & Public Health, University of California, Irvine. She is also Founder and Director of Advocates for African American Elders, a community-led health education, research, and advocacy program dedicated to improving the health, well-being, and quality of life of Black older adults.

A distinguished social worker and sociologist, Dr. Lincoln’s career is defined by a deep commitment to health equity, aging, social justice, and the elimination of disparities affecting Black Americans and underserved communities. Her work bridges rigorous scholarship and public advocacy, translating research into community education, policy change, and practical solutions for those most often excluded from health systems and scientific research.

Dr. Lincoln holds a BA with Honors in Sociology from the University of California, Berkeley, an MSW and MA from the University of Michigan, and a PhD in Social Work and Sociology from the University of Michigan. She is a Fellow of the Gerontological Society of America and a Fellow of the American Academy of Social Work and Social Welfare.

 Her Research

Where Science Meets Social Justice

Social & Environmental Determinants of Health · Aging · Health Disparities

Dr. Lincoln’s research examines how race, racism, socioeconomic inequality, structural disadvantage, environmental exposures, and social determinants of health shape the well-being of Black Americans and other underserved populations. Her scholarship spans social stress, aging, Alzheimer’s disease and brain health disparities, sleep health, women’s health, family caregiving, environmental health equity, and access to care.

At CEHDR, Dr. Lincoln applies an anti-racist and community-engaged research framework to study how environmental exposures, structural racism, and neighborhood-level inequities converge to produce health disparities. Her work includes research on historical redlining, air pollution, climate resilience, racial discrimination, accelerated biological aging, dementia risk, and the physical and mental health consequences of environmental injustice.

She is leading a groundbreaking NIH-funded effort to develop the first Black aging cohort in Southern California, a landmark project designed to address critical gaps in what is known about Alzheimer’s disease, age-related conditions, and health inequities among Black Americans. This work responds to the longstanding underrepresentation of Black communities in clinical and population health research.

 CEHDR Leadership

Leading Multidisciplinary Research for Systemic Change

Director since 2023 · Joe C. Wen School of Population & Public Health

As Director of the Center for Environmental Health Disparities Research, Dr. Lincoln leads a multidisciplinary team of researchers, students, community partners, and policymakers committed to advancing environmental justice and health equity. Through CEHDR, Dr. Lincoln works to build sustainable interventions, strengthen community partnerships, inform policy, and develop the next generation of health equity scholars.  Public Scholarship & Policy Impact

A Trusted Voice in Policy,
Media & Public Health

Public scholarship · Policy leadership · National media engagement

Dr. Lincoln’s leadership extends beyond academia. She has served as a trusted public scholar and policy voice on aging, health disparities, caregiving, racism, and environmental justice. She currently co-chairs California’s Environmental Justice Advisory Council under the Department of Toxic Substances Control, served on California’s Master Plan for Aging Equity Work Group, and has testified before the California State Senate Select Committee on Aging and Long-Term Care.

Her public scholarship has reached audiences across the nation through op-eds in The New York Times, The Wall Street Journal, and StatNews, and through media engagement with outlets including NPR, CNN, ABC News, USA Today, the Los Angeles Times, Forbes, and Black Enterprise. Through this work, Dr. Lincoln consistently uses her platform to name structural racism, economic inequality, environmental injustice, and inadequate access to care as urgent public health issues.

 Founder, AAAE

Advocates for African American Elders

Founded 2012 · Center for Environmental Health Disparities Research, UC Irvine

In 2012, Dr. Lincoln founded Advocates for African American Elders, a community-based research, education, and advocacy program created to improve the health, well-being, and quality of life of Black older adults. Now housed at CEHDR, AAAE works across Los Angeles, Riverside, and Orange Counties to engage older adults, caregivers, families, community leaders, and service providers in health education, research participation, and policy advocacy.

AAAE reflects Dr. Lincoln’s long-standing commitment to meeting communities where they are, building trust, and ensuring that lived experience informs research and policy. The program is a powerful model of community-engaged scholarship, translating research into action for older adults and families who have too often been overlooked by health care, aging services, and public health systems.

 Mentor & Educator

Cultivating the Next Generation

Teaching · Mentorship · Doctoral Training

A dedicated mentor and educator, Dr. Lincoln has helped prepare the next generation of health equity scholars, public health professionals, social workers, and community-engaged researchers. Her mentorship spans doctoral students, postdoctoral fellows, early-career faculty, community health professionals, undergraduate students, MPH students, and CEHDR RISE Fellows.

Her mentoring has taken place through national programs including the NIH Health Policy Research Scholars Program, NIH Research Centers for Minority Aging Research, the Program to Increase Diversity in Behavioral Medicine and Sleep Research, and her own Lincoln Lab at UC Irvine. Many of her former mentees now hold faculty and leadership positions in schools of public health, social work, and related fields, carrying forward research and advocacy that advances health equity in communities across the country.

Dr. Lincoln’s teaching and mentorship reflect the same values that guide her research: equity, rigor, accountability, and a commitment to dismantling the structural roots of health inequality.
