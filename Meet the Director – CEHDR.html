 Who She Is

Driven by Equity. Grounded in Community.

Dr. Karen D. Lincoln is the Director of the Center for Environmental Health Disparities Research (CEHDR) at the Joe C. Wen School of Population & Public Health, University of California, Irvine. She is deeply committed to eliminating environmental racism and advancing policies that promote health, equity, and justice for historically marginalized communities.

 Her Research

Where Science Meets Social Justice

With a distinguished career spanning sociology, social work, gerontology, and public health, Dr. Lincoln's research examines the social and environmental determinants of health — with a focus on the disproportionate burden of exposures on Black and other underserved communities. She champions community-driven solutions to ensure those most impacted have a voice in shaping policy.

CEHDR Leadership

Dr. Lincoln leads a multidisciplinary team of researchers, community partners, and policymakers to develop sustainable interventions that improve health outcomes and drive systemic change across California and beyond.

Founder, AAAE

Dr. Lincoln founded Advocates for African American Elders (AAAE) — a Los Angeles and Orange County organization dedicated to improving health, well-being, and quality of life for Black older adults through education, advocacy, and policy reform.

Mentor & Educator

A celebrated teacher and trainer, Dr. Lincoln has guided countless students and early-career researchers, cultivating a diverse, interdisciplinary workforce committed to advancing social and environmental justice.

 Her Purpose

Education. Empowerment. Action.

The three pillars of Dr. Lincoln's life's work.

Education

Building knowledge across communities, classrooms, and policy spaces.

Empowerment

Centering lived experience and amplifying community voice in research.

Action

Turning evidence into policies and interventions that drive lasting change.

At CEHDR, Dr. Lincoln's work is rooted in a simple but powerful belief: that healthier, more just communities are possible when research, policy, and lived experience come together. Her multidisciplinary approach has produced interventions addressing disparities in healthcare access, environmental exposures, and aging-related inequities — working with local organizations, policymakers, and public health officials to drive lasting systemic change.
